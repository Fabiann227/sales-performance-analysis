2. Business Understanding

The project evaluates marketplace sales performance and compares chronological forecasting strategies to support evidence-based planning. The analytical questions are how sales evolve over time, which products contribute most to performance and error, and which forecasting approach performs best on an untouched future period.

3. Data Understanding

The integrated data contains 41,954 cleaned transactions from January 2024 to December 2025 and 20 products. The modeling table contains 480 product-month observations; zero-sales product-month combinations are retained.

Historical sales

4. Data Preparation

Raw tables were validated, duplicate orders removed, dates and numeric fields coerced, invalid quantity/discount rows excluded, customer ages imputed with the median, missing categorical values filled with Unknown, tables relationally merged, and transaction sales calculated as Quantity × UnitPrice × (1 − Discount).

5. Modeling Methodology

Chronological split: training covers January 2024 to May 2025, validation covers June 2025 to September 2025, and testing covers October 2025 to December 2025. Chronological splitting is required because a random split can place future observations in the training data, producing optimistic estimates and temporal leakage. Models are selected using validation RMSE; the final test period remains separate and is used only for the final comparison.

Expanding-window walk-forward validation

Each fold expands the training window while moving the validation window forward. This tests whether model performance is stable across multiple historical origins instead of relying on one validation window.

6. Model Evaluation

Lower MAE, RMSE, and WAPE are better; higher R² is better. Walk-forward metrics measure repeated validation performance used for model selection. Final-test metrics measure one held-out period after selection. Global strategies apply one model across products, whereas per-product selection allows each product to use its own walk-forward winner.

9. Forecasting Error Analysis

For the best final-test strategy, total absolute error is the sum of absolute errors for a product, MAE is the average absolute error per test observation, and RMSE penalizes larger errors more strongly. Error contribution is a product's share of the strategy's total absolute error. Relative absolute error is defined as total absolute error divided by total actual sales, expressed as a percentage; it is unavailable when actual sales are zero.

10. Business Insights and Recommendations

Measured findings

  • Best walk-forward model: Training Mean Baseline.
  • Best final-test strategy: Global Training Mean Baseline, with WAPE of 13.59%.
  • Products with positive estimated training slopes: Monitor, Bluetooth Speaker, USB-C Cable, Fitness Band, Phone Case, Notebook, Tablet, Mechanical Keyboard, Backpack, Headphones.
  • Products with negative estimated training slopes: Smart Watch, Microphone, Gaming Controller, Desk Lamp, Power Bank, Office Chair, Webcam, Laptop Stand, Wireless Mouse, HDMI Cable.
  • Largest absolute-error contributors under the best strategy: Smart Watch, Office Chair, Headphones, Tablet, Microphone.

Recommendations

  • Use Global Training Mean Baseline as the aggregate reference strategy for the evaluated final-test horizon.
  • Treat positive and negative fitted slopes as descriptive summaries rather than statistically established trends.
  • Review products with high relative error separately from products with high absolute error before using forecasts for planning.
  • Refresh the analysis when new completed transactions are available and re-evaluate the strategy chronologically.

11. Conclusion and Limitations

The pipeline provides a reproducible view of historical sales, chronological model validation, final-test evaluation, product-level model selection, and future estimates. The historical period is limited to 24 months, the final test period to three months, and the models primarily use time-index features rather than external drivers. Forecast generation is an analytical output and does not represent production deployment.
